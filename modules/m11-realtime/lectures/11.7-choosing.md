# 11.7 Choosing the right tool

## Hook (2 min)

"Let's make everything real-time." Then 2,000 readers each hold a socket all evening to watch an unread
counter that changes twice an hour.

## Concept (15 min)

Four questions per feature:

1. **How stale may it be?** Sub-second (chat), seconds (a counter), next page view (recommendations).
2. **How often does it change while someone watches?** Constantly (typing), sometimes (chat), rarely (a
   badge).
3. **Who watches, and how many at once?** Tens per room, thousands on a home screen, one partner server.
4. **What happens when the connection drops?** Is a missed event lost, harmless, or recovered by a query?

| Option | Good for | Cost grows with | On failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Query (cached) | data needed once per view | requests | retry |
| Polling | staleness of seconds or more, large idle audiences | clients × poll rate | nothing special |
| Subscription | low latency, frequent events for the people watching | sockets + subscribers × events | reconnect, fill the gap by query |
| `@defer` | one slow part of a screen | nothing extra | the part fails alone (`completed` with errors) |
| Live query | "this screen, always current" | result size × changes | re-query |
| Webhook | other servers, offline receivers | events × partners, retries | retries, idempotency |
| CDC | the source of events, whatever the transport | database changes | the slot holds WAL until read |

Real-time is a cost you pay per watcher. Spend it where a human notices the delay.

## Java build (20 min)

Lab 11.7 has no code: a decision matrix for six Shelfie features with each choice's runner-up and costs,
using your own numbers from labs 11.5 and 11.6.

## Experiment (15 min)

Defend one choice in a two-minute pitch to someone who prefers the runner-up (group: everyone pitches a
different row, the others vote).

## Check

1. Which grows faster with an idle audience: polling's cost or a subscription's?
2. When is polling the better choice even though subscriptions are faster?
3. Why is CDC in the table though no client ever speaks it?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Polling's, which pays every interval whether or not anything happened; an idle subscription costs a
   socket and keep-alives.
2. When seconds of staleness are fine and the audience is large and mostly idle (the unread badge), or the
   client cannot hold connections.
3. It decides where events come from (every committed write), and feeds subscriptions, webhooks or caches.
