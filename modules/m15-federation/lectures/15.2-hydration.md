# 15.2 Stitching and hydration

## Hook (2 min)

Before federation, gateways glued schemas together themselves: take two services that know nothing of each other,
merge their schemas, and teach the gateway that `Review.bookId` means "ask catalog". Atlassian still runs its graph
this way, on the JVM, with Nadel.

## Concept (15 min)

### Stitching

Schema stitching (graphql-tools' name): the gateway holds an **overall schema** built from each service's
**underlying schema**, plus the links between them. Services stay plain GraphQL. The gateway owns the joins, the
renames, the hidden fields.

### Nadel

[Nadel](https://github.com/atlassian-labs/nadel) (Atlassian, Apache-2.0, Kotlin; Maven Central
`com.atlassian:nadel`, pinned 0.42134418907, released 2026-11-06; 0.44642751879 of 2026-11-20 was probed but is too new to pin) is a library: you give it overall and underlying
SDL per service and a `ServiceExecution` per service (how to send it a document), and call
`nadel.execute(NadelExecutionInput)`. It ships its own graphql-java build, so it lives outside Spring.

- `@hydrated(service: "catalog", field: "books", arguments: [{ name: "ids", value: "$source.bookId" }],
  identifiedBy: "id")`: a field filled by calling another service. With a list field and `identifiedBy`, Nadel
  **batches and de-duplicates**: probed, five reviews over three books were one `books(ids: [3 ids])` call;
  pointed at `book(id:)` instead, four reviews were four calls, a duplicate included.
- `@renamed(from: "title")`: the gateway renames a field; the old name is gone for clients, the service unchanged
  (Nadel sends `rename__name__title: title`).
- Fields not in the overall schema (`bookId`) are hidden but still fetchable by the gateway.

### Who owns the link

In stitching, the gateway team. In federation (15.3), the subgraph that declares `Review.book: Book` with Book's
key. Stitching fits many existing services you cannot change and a central API team; federation fits teams that
own their slice of the graph.
