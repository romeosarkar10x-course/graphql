# 15.7 Operating a federated graph

## Hook (2 min)

Moving `Review.book` from catalog to reviews looks like a two-line change. Done in the wrong order, under traffic
every 50 ms, it failed 193 of 478 requests. Done in four deploys, 638 requests, zero errors.

## Concept (15 min)

### Moving a field

1. **Claim**: reviews adds `book` with `@override(from: "catalog")`. Composes; the router asks reviews.
2. **Remove**: catalog drops its `Review` stub. Composes; nothing asked catalog for it anymore.
3. **Tidy**: reviews removes `@override`.

One team per deploy; each step composes; each can be rolled back until the old code is gone. Progressive
override (`@override(from: "catalog", label: "percent(25)")`) routes a share of requests to the new owner first;
both routers support `percent(N)`, Hive also expressions in `override_labels`.

### Blue/green and hot reload

The router reloads the supergraph file (`--hot-reload` on Apollo; Hive polls with `supergraph.poll_interval`).
Probed: 200 queries during a reload, 0 errors. A subgraph deploy: start the new version beside the old one,
compose a supergraph pointing at it, let the router reload, stop the old one.

### Headers and identity

Each subgraph authorizes on its own (Module 10): the router forwards `Authorization` (and here `X-Shelfie-User`),
nothing else. A subgraph that also trusts calls not from the router needs the network to enforce that.

### Ownership in practice

A runbook per change type, composition in every subgraph's CI, a test suite run through the router against the
composed graph (mini-project 15.8 shows what only it catches: headers, behaviour, data).

## Java build (20 min)

Lab 15.7: the states and `RUNBOOK.md`; the grader deploys them blue/green under traffic.

## Experiment (15 min)

Move `Review.book` with zero errors. Then swap two steps and watch the error count.

## Check

1. Why not remove the field from catalog in the same deploy as the claim?
2. When is `percent(N)` worth it?
3. What can roll back after step 2?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Two teams' deploys are never atomic; whichever lands first, the other state must compose and serve.
2. When the new resolver's behaviour or load is uncertain: route a share, watch errors and latency, raise it.
3. Step 2 itself (catalog restores its stub, the override still wins); undoing step 1 then needs catalog first.

</details>
