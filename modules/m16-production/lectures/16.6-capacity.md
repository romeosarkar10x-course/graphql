# 16.6 Capacity and cost

## Hook (2 min)

One partner query (`books(first: 10)` with two levels of `similar` and reviews) costs 1,871 by Shelfie's
rule. A club page costs 61. Counted as requests they look alike, until the partner sends 80 a second. Scale on what
requests cost, and know where your latency curve breaks.

## Concept (15 min)

### Static cost

Count each field as 1, times the number of items its parents return: `first` when the list has it, otherwise an
expected size. The cost is known before execution (Module 10.4 used the same idea to reject queries). As a metric
(`graphql_cost_sum` by client and operation) it is a load signal that knows a heavy query from a light one.

### Autoscaling on it

A HorizontalPodAutoscaler (`autoscaling/v2`) scales on a Pods metric with an `AverageValue` target. The
algorithm, from the Kubernetes documentation:

- every 15 s, `desired = ceil(current × metric / target)`;
- no change within a 0.1 tolerance;
- scale-up adds at most max(4 pods, 100%) per 15 s;
- scale-down takes the highest recommendation of the last 300 s.

An adapter (prometheus-adapter, KEDA) turns `sum by (instance) (rate(graphql_cost_sum[30s]))` into
`graphql_cost_per_second`.

### The target is a trade-off

Simulated over a ramp from 1.5 to 6 pods' worth of traffic, with new pods taking 30 s to serve:

| Target | Seconds overloaded | Pod-seconds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 37% of capacity | 0 | 13,002 |
| 75% | 15 | 7,814 |
| 98% | 214 | 5,970 |

Headroom pays for reaction time. Too much headroom pays for idle pods.

### Finding capacity: the knee

Raise the rate until p99 breaks. One CPU (pinned, as a pod limit would), club pages, probed:

| Rate (pages/s) | p99 |
| --- | --- |
| up to 600 | under 40 ms |
| 800 | 86 ms |
| 1000 | 380 ms |

The knee is about 700 pages a second, so 42,700 cost per second per pod. Parsing and validating each distinct
document once moved the knee to about 1,000; at 1,200, every request timed out. Curves end in cliffs, so keep the
target well below the knee.

### Cheaper than more pods

- **Edge caching**: public, identical answers (persisted GET queries, Module 9) served by a CDN never reach the
  pods.
- **Cost-aware throttling**: a budget per client, enforced before execution.
- **Multi-region reads**: replicas near users, at the price of capacity per region and replicated dependencies.

## Check

1. Why is cost per second a better autoscaling signal than CPU for this API?
2. Why not set the target at 100% of the measured capacity?
3. What does the 300 s scale-down window protect against, and what does it cost?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. It moves with the expensive queries before the CPU average does, and it is known before execution.
2. New pods take time; at 98% the ramp outran the scaler for 214 s.
3. Flapping on short dips; you pay for extra pods for five minutes after a peak.
