# 14.2 Locales, time zones, money

## Hook (2 min)

One meetup at 18:30 UTC. New York reads Thursday 1:30 PM, Berlin 19:30, Tokyo Friday 3:30 AM. A price of 12.50 EUR
is `€12.50` in English and `12,50 €` in German, and the space before `€` is not a space. The server stores one
instant and one decimal; everything else is rendering.

## Concept (15 min)

### Language: Accept-Language, every range

[RFC 9110 §12.5.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110#section-12.5.4): a list of ranges with weights. Shelfie
negotiates content (which blurb) and separately formats (numbers, dates). Spring for GraphQL hands a controller one
`Locale`: probed, the first range (`de-DE,de;q=0.9` gives `de_DE`), and **the JVM's default** (`en_US` in the
course image) when there is no header. That is not negotiation: `es, de;q=0.8` must give German text when there is
no Spanish. The JDK does RFC 4647 for you: `Locale.LanguageRange.parse` (sorted by weight, throws on garbage) and
`Locale.lookupTag` (fr-CH falls back to fr). Pass the ranges through the `GraphQLContext` from an interceptor.
Localised fields can also take an argument (`blurb(language:)`): explicit, cacheable, but every client must pass it.

### Time: instants and zones

Store `Instant` (UTC). A zone changes only how it is written: `instant.atZone(ZoneId.of("Europe/Berlin"))`.
extended-scalars `DateTime` (24.0, works on graphql-java 26.1) always carries an offset: probed output
`2026-12-03T19:30:00.000+01:00`. `Date` is a calendar day, which depends on the zone (m3 is 31 December in New
York, 1 January in Tokyo). Durations are real time: two hours from 01:30+01:00 on the night the clocks go forward
end at 04:30+02:00, not 03:30. Zone ids are IANA names; an unknown one is the client's mistake (`BAD_REQUEST`).

### Money: amount plus currency

A Float cannot hold 0.10; sums drift. Send a decimal string and an ISO 4217 code; validate input against
`Currency.getDefaultFractionDigits()` (yen 0, euro 2). `formatted` is a convenience by the JDK's CLDR data, and
CLDR is precise: probed, German uses a no-break space (U+00A0) before `€`, French groups thousands with a narrow
no-break space (U+202F), and English times put U+202F before `AM`. Tests that compare typed strings fail on them.

## Java build (20 min)

Lab 14.2: an interceptor that parses Accept-Language into the context; blurbs by lookup, formats by the first
range; `startsAt(timeZone:)`, `endsAt`, `localDate`, `startsAtText`; `Money` and a validated `setPrice`.

## Experiment (15 min)

Request the same book in 3 locales and 3 time zones: the grader prints the 3x3 table for meetup m1. Then the DST
meetup in Berlin, and `Accept-Language: es, de;q=0.8`.

## Check

1. What does Spring's `Locale` argument give you with no Accept-Language header?
2. Why is `endsAt` computed from the instant and not from a local date-time?
3. Why `amount: String!` and not `amount: Float!`?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. The JVM's default locale, which is the server's, not the reader's (`en_US` in the course image).
2. Local wall-clock time skips or repeats an hour when the clocks change; adding minutes to the instant measures
   real time, and the zone only renders the result.
3. A binary float cannot represent most decimal amounts exactly and loses cents in arithmetic; a decimal string
   with its currency is exact, and clients parse it into their own decimal type.

</details>
