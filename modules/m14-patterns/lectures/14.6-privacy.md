# 14.6 Auditing, soft delete, privacy

## Hook (2 min)

"Delete my account." Their row is gone. Their reviews are still there, their email is in last week's log lines,
their profile is in a cache, and the audit log names them on every row. GDPR Article 17 says erase; where is
everything?

## Concept (15 min)

### Who changed what: audit in one place

An audit trail must not depend on every resolver remembering it. A graphql-java `Instrumentation` sees every
execution: in `instrumentExecutionResult`, parse the document, take the operation, and record each top-level
mutation field with a time, an actor id, the operation name, the argument **names** and the outcome (an error whose
path starts with the field's result key). Not the values: a review body or a search term can be personal data,
and audit logs are kept longest of all. Failed attempts are audited too; they are the interesting ones.

### Soft delete and retention

`deleted_at` hides a row from every read at once and makes "undo" possible. The cost: every read path filters it,
forever (one forgotten `deleted_at IS NULL` resurrects data). A retention job turns soft into hard after a period
(30 days in lab 14.6, a nightly `@Scheduled` plus an admin mutation). Undo windows are a product decision;
retention periods are often a legal one.

### Erasure: personal data is everywhere

Places personal data hides, and what to do:

| Place | Erasure |
|---|---|
| the user row, content they wrote | delete in one transaction (soft-deleted rows too) |
| audit and history tables | keep the event, break the link (actor `erased`, or a pseudonym whose key is destroyed) |
| caches | evict on erase; a cache is a copy |
| logs | never write it: log ids, not names, emails or text |
| search indexes, exports, data warehouse | delete by id, downstream |
| backups | expire on schedule; document it |
| event streams | compaction with tombstones, or encrypt per user and destroy the key (crypto-shredding) |

### PII masking

Lab 14.1's `@mask`, with one more rule: the owner sees their own data whole. The directive reads the viewer from the
`GraphQLContext` and the user from the source object. Masking in the API is not erasure: the data is still stored.

## Java build (20 min)

Lab 14.6: the audit `Instrumentation`, soft delete with restore and purge, `deleteMyAccount` across tables, audit
and cache, logs that name ids only, `@mask` for non-owners.

## Experiment (15 min)

Run an erase request; verify nothing personal remains in DB, logs or caches: the grader warms every cache, erases
Oluwaseun, then searches the API, every table of the lab's schema as text, the audit log and the app's output.

## Check

1. Why does the audit store argument names but not values?
2. A user is erased. The audit log has 40 rows with their user id. What should happen to them?
3. Why is "no PII in logs" a design rule rather than something an erase can fix?

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Values can be personal data (free text, search terms, addresses), and the audit is kept longer and copied more
   than anything else; names say what was done, which is what an audit needs.
2. Keep the events (what happened, when, outcome) and remove the link to the person: rewrite the actor to a marker
   such as `erased`.
3. Logs are shipped, indexed and archived in systems the app does not control; once written, the app cannot find
   and delete every copy.

</details>
